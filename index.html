Reminders that wait until you are actually there

A repeating reminder counts working seconds, not wall-clock ones. Step away and the clock pauses instead of resetting — so a 30-minute water reminder can never go off while you are at lunch.

An ordinary timer

Press it and watch the two clocks disagree. That disagreement is the whole feature — it is why this reminder is still installed in a month.

Banked time survives a restart, too. It is written to disk every minute, so quitting the app does not silently wipe your progress toward a break.

One-off alarms work the other way round, on the wall clock: Remind me in… → 2 hours, type what it should say, done. It fires whether you are at the desk or not, chimes, and deletes itself.

A notification you cannot miss, and cannot lose

Banner notifications slide away while you are mid-sentence and end up in a tray you never open. Hangabout comes down the screen, swings to catch your eye, and then simply stays there holding the message until you deal with it.

Click it to dismiss

One click on the companion and the reminder is done.

Click the balloon to snooze

Five more minutes. The balloon says so in small type, because a snooze you have to discover is a snooze nobody uses.

One at a time

Anything else that comes due keeps its place in the queue and arrives once you have dealt with the first.

Focus sessions that do not become the distraction

Pick Focus → 25 minutes and a small countdown chip pins itself above the companion. Click the character to pause, click again to resume.

The session deliberately does not move the character at all. An earlier version sank it down the screen as the time ran out, which was a nice idea and exactly wrong: it moved the one thing you were trying to ignore, on purpose, for half an hour.

Repeating reminders go quiet during a session. One-off alarms do not — an alarm is tied to a moment that passes, and swallowing one silently would be worse than the interruption. Working time still banks while they are muted, so a focus session never erases your progress toward a break.

It knows when to disappear

The companion spends nearly all its time tucked under the top edge of the screen, and — more importantly — it stops moving.

Something that sways all day teaches you to ignore it, and then it cannot tell you anything. Stillness is what gives the movement meaning, so there is no idle fidgeting while it is tucked away.

Hide while typing pulls it completely out of sight whenever a key has been pressed in the last two and a half seconds. And the window it lives in passes every click straight through to whatever is underneath — it only becomes grabbable in the moment your cursor is actually on it, so a full-width invisible panel sits over your screen without ever eating a click.

Pick who keeps you company

Six companions ship with the app, on two kinds of motion: some hang from a thread at the top of the screen, others stand on the floor and walk over when they have something to say.

Spider

Hangs from a thread. Drag it down and the silk stretches, then it hauls itself back up in pulls.

Cat and Dog

Asleep in a corner until there is news, then they get up and walk over. Both drawn in code.

Iron Man and Tony Stark

One stands on the floor and lights the thrusters only when he is going somewhere. The other idles out of the armour and suits up when there is something to do.

Captain America

Stands at ease, and throws the shield. It glances off two screen edges, buries itself in a third with a quiver, and comes home when the balloon does.

The menu only ever offers what makes sense: “give it a flick” means nothing to a man who flies, so it is not offered to him.

You can add your own, too. Drop a folder of images and a small character.json into the app’s Characters folder and it appears in the menu alongside the rest — no code, no rebuild.

Getting it running

  1. Download the zip and double-click it.
  2. Drag Hangabout.app into your Applications folder.
  3. Double-click it. macOS will refuse the first time — see below.

macOS will say it “cannot verify Hangabout is free of malware”.

That is not a warning about this app. macOS says it about anything that has not been through Apple’s $99/year notarization, and Hangabout is a hobby project.

Click Done, then open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll to the bottom and click Open Anyway. Authenticate, confirm once more, and that is it — only ever the first time.

A companion appears at the top of your screen and a hanger icon appears in your menu bar. Everything lives in that menu. There is no Dock icon, by design. To remove it: quit from the menu bar and drag the app to the Trash — it leaves nothing behind.
